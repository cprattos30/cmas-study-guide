# FATF Recommendations & Interpretive Notes -- CAMS Reference Guide

> **Study Priority:** The CAMS Study Guide (Appendix, p. 393) explicitly states: *"Two documents above all others (FATF's 40 Recommendations and Interpretive Notes) should receive particular study from CAMS candidates."* This chapter is your comprehensive reference to both.

---

## 1. What Is the FATF?

The **Financial Action Task Force (FATF)** is an inter-governmental policy-making body established in 1989 by the G-7 Summit in Paris. Its mandate is to set standards and promote effective implementation of legal, regulatory, and operational measures for combating **money laundering (ML)**, **terrorist financing (TF)**, **proliferation financing (PF)**, and other related threats to the integrity of the international financial system.

### Key Facts for the Exam

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 1989, by the G-7 |
| **Headquarters** | Paris, France (housed at the OECD) |
| **Members** | 40 members (38 jurisdictions + 2 regional organizations: the European Commission and the Gulf Co-operation Council) |
| **Global Network** | Over 200 jurisdictions committed via FATF + 9 FATF-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs) |
| **Core Output** | The 40 Recommendations (first issued 1990; comprehensively revised 2012; continuously updated) |
| **Current Standards** | Updated through October 2025 (includes Feb 2025 financial inclusion amendments, June 2025 R.16 revision) |
| **Evaluation Cycle** | Currently in the 5th Round of Mutual Evaluations (began 2024; 6-year cycle) |

### FATF-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs)

The 9 FSRBs extend the FATF's reach globally. Know these for the exam:

- **APG** -- Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering
- **CFATF** -- Caribbean Financial Action Task Force
- **EAG** -- Eurasian Group
- **ESAAMLG** -- Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group
- **GAFILAT** -- Financial Action Task Force of Latin America
- **GIABA** -- Inter-Governmental Action Group against Money Laundering in West Africa
- **MENAFATF** -- Middle East and North Africa Financial Action Task Force
- **MONEYVAL** -- Committee of Experts on the Evaluation of Anti-Money Laundering Measures (Council of Europe)
- **GABAC** -- Task Force on Money Laundering in Central Africa

## 2. What Are the Interpretive Notes?

The FATF Standards comprise three components:

1. **The 40 Recommendations** -- the high-level principles and obligations
2. **The Interpretive Notes (INRs)** -- detailed, binding guidance that explains *how* to implement each Recommendation (these have the same binding force as the Recommendations themselves)
3. **The Glossary** -- definitions of key terms used throughout the Standards

> **Key Exam Point:** The Interpretive Notes are NOT optional guidance. They carry the same weight as the Recommendations themselves and are assessed during mutual evaluations. When the CAMS exam references a "FATF standard," it means the Recommendation AND its Interpretive Note together.

The Interpretive Notes specify the *operational detail* -- the "who, what, when, and how" -- that turns a broad principle into a concrete compliance obligation. Not all 40 Recommendations have Interpretive Notes, but the most critical ones (R.1, R.3, R.5, R.6, R.7, R.8, R.10, R.13, R.14, R.15, R.16, R.18, R.19, R.20, R.22, R.24, R.25, R.26, R.29) do.

## 4. Group A -- AML/CFT Policies and Coordination (R.1-R.2)

### Recommendation 1: Assessing Risks and Applying a Risk-Based Approach ★★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should identify, assess, and understand the ML/TF/PF risks they face, and should apply a **risk-based approach (RBA)** to ensure that measures to prevent or mitigate ML/TF are **proportionate** to the risks identified.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.1):**

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **National Risk Assessment (NRA)** | Countries must conduct an NRA and keep it up to date; designate an authority or mechanism to coordinate the NRA |
| **Higher-Risk Situations** | When risks are higher, countries must ensure FIs and DNFBPs apply **enhanced measures** |
| **Lower-Risk Situations** | Countries must **allow and encourage simplified measures** when risk is lower (strengthened in Feb 2025 amendment) |
| **Scope of AML/CFT Regime** | Countries may extend their regime to additional institutions, sectors, or activities if they pose higher ML/TF risk |
| **Supervisory Oversight** | Supervisors must review risk profiles and risk assessments prepared by FIs and DNFBPs |
| **Documentation** | Risk assessments must be documented and kept up to date |

**February 2025 Amendment -- Financial Inclusion Changes:**
- The word **"commensurate"** was replaced with **"proportionate"** throughout the Standards, clarifying that measures should appropriately correspond to identified risk levels
- Explicit requirement for countries to **allow and encourage simplified measures** in lower-risk scenarios
- Non-face-to-face transactions are only considered potentially higher-risk where **appropriate risk mitigation measures have not been implemented** (recognizing that digital interaction is now standard)
- Corresponding amendments were also made to INR.10 and INR.15

> **Key Exam Point:** R.1 is the FOUNDATION of the entire FATF framework. The risk-based approach is not optional -- it is mandatory. Both over-compliance (applying excessive measures to low-risk situations, harming financial inclusion) and under-compliance (failing to apply enhanced measures to high-risk situations) are deficiencies. The FATF expects a calibrated, proportionate response.

**Why It Matters Practically:** Every compliance decision -- from how much CDD to apply, to how frequently to monitor, to how many resources to allocate to a particular product line -- must be grounded in a documented risk assessment. The RBA is tested across every domain of the CAMS exam.

### Recommendation 2: National Cooperation and Coordination

Countries should have national AML/CFT/CPF policies informed by identified risks, and should establish mechanisms for cooperation and coordination between policy-makers, the FIU, law enforcement, supervisors, and other relevant authorities.

## 5. Group B -- Money Laundering and Confiscation (R.3-R.4)

### Recommendation 3: Money Laundering Offense ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should criminalize money laundering on the basis of the **Vienna Convention** (1988) and the **Palermo Convention** (2000). The offense should apply to **all serious offences**, with a view to including the **widest range of predicate offences**.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.3):**

- ML should extend to **any type of property**, regardless of value, that directly or indirectly represents proceeds of crime
- The **intent and knowledge** required to prove ML can be inferred from **objective factual circumstances**
- Predicate offences should include conduct committed in **other countries** (dual criminality is not required if the conduct would constitute a predicate offence domestically)
- Countries should ensure that **ancillary offences** (attempt, aiding, abetting, counseling, facilitating) are criminalized
- ML should be treated as applicable to persons who commit the predicate offence (**self-laundering**)
- **Effective, proportionate, and dissuasive** criminal sanctions must apply

**The FATF's 21 Designated Categories of Predicate Offences:**

| # | Category | # | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Participation in an organized criminal group & racketeering | 12 | Environmental crime |
| 2 | Terrorism, including terrorist financing | 13 | Murder, grievous bodily injury |
| 3 | Trafficking in human beings & migrant smuggling | 14 | Kidnapping, illegal restraint & hostage-taking |
| 4 | Sexual exploitation (incl. of children) | 15 | Robbery or theft |
| 5 | Illicit trafficking in narcotic drugs & psychotropic substances | 16 | Smuggling (incl. customs/excise/tax evasion) |
| 6 | Illicit arms trafficking | 17 | Tax crimes (direct and indirect taxes) |
| 7 | Illicit trafficking in stolen & other goods | 18 | Extortion |
| 8 | Corruption & bribery | 19 | Forgery |
| 9 | Fraud | 20 | Piracy |
| 10 | Counterfeiting currency | 21 | Insider trading & market manipulation |
| 11 | Counterfeiting & piracy of products | | |

> **Key Exam Point:** The CAMS exam frequently tests knowledge of predicate offences. Remember: the list is deliberately broad. Countries can choose a **threshold approach** (all offences above a certain penalty threshold) or a **list approach** (specifying categories), but either way must cover these 21 categories at minimum.

### Recommendation 4: Confiscation and Provisional Measures

Countries should adopt measures to enable the confiscation of proceeds of crime, property of corresponding value, instrumentalities used or intended for use in ML/TF offences, and property laundered. This includes provisional measures (freezing/seizing) to prevent dissipation of assets.

## 6. Group C -- Terrorist Financing and Financing of Proliferation (R.5-R.8)

### Recommendation 5: Terrorist Financing Offense ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should criminalize terrorist financing on the basis of the **International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism** (1999).

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.5):**

- Countries must criminalize the financing of **terrorist acts**, **terrorist organizations**, AND **individual terrorists** -- even in the **absence of a link to a specific terrorist act**
- The offence should extend to **any person** who wilfully provides or collects funds by **any means**, directly or indirectly
- The offence covers funds from **both legitimate and illegitimate sources**
- It should **NOT** be required that the funds were actually used to carry out a terrorist act or be linked to a specific act
- **Effective, proportionate, and dissuasive criminal sanctions** must apply to natural persons
- Criminal liability should apply to **legal persons** (corporations) as well, with criminal, civil, or administrative sanctions

> **Key Exam Point:** The crucial distinction from R.3: TF does not require proceeds of crime. Funds can be entirely **legitimate** in origin. The crime is the *purpose* (financing terrorism), not the *source*. Also, no link to a specific attack is needed -- financing a terrorist organization is sufficient.

### Recommendation 6: Targeted Financial Sanctions Related to Terrorism and TF ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries must implement **targeted financial sanctions (TFS)** without delay to comply with UN Security Council Resolutions relating to the prevention and suppression of terrorism and terrorist financing.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.6):**

The INR.6 covers two distinct UNSCR regimes:

| UNSCR Regime | Scope | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **UNSCR 1267 (1999)** and successors | Al-Qaeda, ISIL (Da'esh), and associated persons/entities | Designations made by the **UN Security Council** (the 1267 Committee) |
| **UNSCR 1373 (2001)** | Broader: any person/entity involved in terrorist acts, financing, or facilitation | Each **country designates** based on its own domestic processes |

**Core obligations:**
- **Freeze without delay** the funds and other assets of designated persons/entities
- **Prohibit** making funds or other assets available to designated persons/entities
- Implement **de-listing procedures** and mechanisms for unfreezing
- Permit access to frozen funds necessary for **basic expenses** (under certain conditions)
- Communicate designations to the **financial sector** immediately

> **Key Exam Point:** "Without delay" is a key phrase. The FATF does not define a specific timeframe but expects the freeze to occur as close to immediately as possible. Many jurisdictions fail mutual evaluations on the timeliness of implementation.

### Recommendation 7: Targeted Financial Sanctions Related to Proliferation ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries must implement targeted financial sanctions to comply with UNSCRs relating to the prevention, suppression, and disruption of **proliferation of weapons of mass destruction (WMD)** and its financing.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.7):**

- Parallels R.6 but specifically targets **proliferation financing** (currently focused on **DPRK** and **Iran**)
- Countries must freeze **without delay** the funds/assets of designated persons and entities
- Prohibit making funds available to designated persons/entities
- Establish mechanisms for de-listing, unfreezing, and granting exemptions for humanitarian purposes
- Monitor and enforce compliance through competent authorities

> **Key Exam Point:** R.7 was significantly strengthened in the 2012 revision. The FATF also added R.1's risk-based approach to proliferation financing (CPF) in the October 2020 amendments. Know the distinction: R.6 = terrorism; R.7 = proliferation (WMD).

### Recommendation 8: Non-Profit Organizations ★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should review the adequacy of their laws and regulations relating to NPOs that the country has identified as being vulnerable to terrorist financing abuse, and should apply **focused, proportionate, and risk-based measures** to protect them from TF abuse.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.8):**

- R.8 applies only to organizations falling within the **FATF definition of NPO** (organizations that raise or disburse funds for charitable, religious, cultural, educational, social, or fraternal purposes)
- NOT the entire universe of non-profit entities -- only those assessed as **vulnerable to TF abuse**
- Countries must **periodically identify** which NPOs fall within the definition and assess TF risks
- Countries should **NOT** designate and supervise NPOs as reporting entities or require them to conduct CDD
- Measures must not **unduly disrupt** legitimate NPO activities
- Outreach to the NPO sector is essential

> **Key Exam Point:** The revised R.8 is a case study in proportionality. The FATF recognized that many countries were over-applying R.8, stifling legitimate civil society. The exam may test your understanding that R.8 is about **protecting** NPOs from abuse, not treating them as inherently suspicious.

## 7. Group D -- Preventive Measures (R.9-R.23)

This is the largest and most CAMS-critical group. It contains the obligations that financial institutions and DNFBPs must implement day to day.

### Recommendation 9: Financial Institution Secrecy Laws

Countries should ensure that financial institution secrecy laws do **not** inhibit the implementation of the FATF Recommendations. This is a foundational enabler for all other preventive measures.

---

### Recommendation 10: Customer Due Diligence ★★★★★

**This is the single most important Recommendation for the CAMS exam.**

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Financial institutions should be prohibited from keeping **anonymous accounts** or accounts in **obviously fictitious names**. FIs should be required to undertake CDD measures when: establishing business relationships; carrying out occasional transactions above the threshold; there is a suspicion of ML/TF; or the FI doubts the veracity of previously obtained identification data.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.10):**

#### When CDD Must Be Applied

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Establishing a **business relationship** | Always |
| **Occasional transactions** above the threshold | **USD/EUR 15,000** (single or linked) |
| Wire transfers covered by R.16 | Per INR.16 thresholds |
| **Suspicion** of ML/TF | Regardless of amount |
| **Doubts** about veracity/adequacy of prior CDD | Regardless of amount |

#### The Four Elements of CDD

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **(a) Identify and verify the customer** | Using reliable, independent source documents, data, or information |
| **(b) Identify the beneficial owner** | Take reasonable measures to verify identity using reliable information; understand ownership and control structure |
| **(c) Understand the purpose and intended nature** | Of the business relationship |
| **(d) Conduct ongoing due diligence** | Ongoing monitoring of transactions and updating CDD information |

#### Beneficial Ownership -- The Key Definition

The **beneficial owner** is the natural person(s) who ultimately **owns or controls** a customer and/or the natural person on whose behalf a transaction is being conducted. For legal persons, this includes:
- The natural person(s) who ultimately owns/controls (directly or indirectly) **25% or more** of the shares/voting rights (many jurisdictions use this threshold)
- Where no natural person meets the above, the natural person(s) who exercises control through other means
- Where no natural person is identified, the **senior managing official**

#### Simplified Due Diligence (SDD) vs. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

| Aspect | Simplified (SDD) | Enhanced (EDD) |
|---|---|---|
| **When applied** | Lower-risk situations identified through risk assessment | Higher-risk situations (PEPs, correspondent banking, high-risk countries, complex structures, etc.) |
| **Measures** | Reduced frequency of CDD updates; simplified verification; less intensive monitoring | Additional information obtained; more detailed scrutiny; senior management approval; enhanced ongoing monitoring |
| **Key principle** | SDD is NOT an exemption from CDD -- it is a reduced application | EDD is NOT a specific checklist -- it means doing MORE to understand and mitigate the higher risk |
| **Feb 2025 update** | Countries are now **required** to allow and encourage SDD | Remains mandatory for specified higher-risk scenarios |

#### Timing of Verification

- Verification should be completed **before or during** the establishment of the business relationship
- If necessary to **not interrupt normal business**, verification may be completed **after** establishing the relationship, provided: (a) the ML/TF risk is effectively managed, (b) it occurs as soon as reasonably practicable
- This flexibility is particularly relevant for **non-face-to-face** relationships (which, per the Feb 2025 update, are no longer automatically categorized as higher risk if proper mitigation is in place)

#### Existing Customers

- CDD must be applied to existing customers on the basis of **materiality and risk**
- Institutions must conduct due diligence at **appropriate times**, taking into account when CDD was previously done and the adequacy of data obtained

#### Failure to Complete CDD

If the FI cannot satisfactorily complete CDD:
- It should **NOT open the account**, commence the business relationship, or perform the transaction
- It should **consider making a suspicious transaction report (STR)**
- It should **terminate** an existing business relationship

> **Key Exam Point:** R.10/INR.10 is the beating heart of AML compliance. Expect multiple exam questions on: the four CDD elements, when CDD must be applied (especially the USD/EUR 15,000 threshold), beneficial ownership identification, the distinction between SDD and EDD, and the consequences of failing to complete CDD. The Feb 2025 changes promoting proportionality and SDD are likely to appear on recent exams.

### Recommendation 11: Record Keeping ★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
FIs should maintain all necessary records on transactions (domestic and international) for at least **five years**, to enable swift reconstruction of individual transactions and to provide evidence for criminal prosecution.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.11):**

- **Transaction records** must be kept for at least **5 years after the transaction**
- **CDD information** (identification data, account files, business correspondence, results of analysis) must be kept for at least **5 years after the end of the business relationship**
- Records must be **sufficient to reconstruct individual transactions**, including amounts and types of currency
- Records must be available to **domestic competent authorities** upon appropriate authority
- Countries may set **longer** retention periods

> **Key Exam Point:** "Five years" is a constant on the CAMS exam. Transaction records = 5 years from the transaction. CDD/identity records = 5 years from the END of the business relationship. This is a minimum -- many jurisdictions require longer.

### Recommendation 12: Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) ★★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
FIs should have measures in place to determine if customers or beneficial owners are PEPs, and if so, apply enhanced due diligence measures.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.12):**

#### PEP Categories and Requirements

| PEP Type | Enhanced Measures Required |
|---|---|
| **Foreign PEPs** | Always: (a) risk-management systems to identify; (b) senior management approval; (c) establish source of wealth and source of funds; (d) enhanced ongoing monitoring |
| **Domestic PEPs** | Risk-based: where there is a higher-risk business relationship, same measures as foreign PEPs |
| **International organization PEPs** | Risk-based: where there is a higher-risk business relationship, same measures as foreign PEPs |
| **Family members and close associates** of all PEPs | Same enhanced measures apply |

#### Key Definitions

- **PEP:** An individual entrusted with a **prominent public function** (heads of state, senior politicians, senior government officials, judicial/military officials, senior executives of state-owned enterprises, senior political party officials)
- **Family members:** Spouse, children, parents, siblings, and in-laws
- **Close associates:** Individuals with joint beneficial ownership or close business relationships, or who are nominee beneficial owners of legal entities

#### Important Principles

- PEP requirements are **preventive**, NOT criminal -- being a PEP does not mean being a criminal
- The definition includes persons **currently** holding a prominent public function AND those who **formerly** held such functions (with a risk-based determination of when to downgrade)
- Insurance policies: beneficiaries who are PEPs should be identified no later than the time of **payout**

> **Key Exam Point:** Foreign PEPs ALWAYS require EDD. Domestic and international organization PEPs require EDD only when the business relationship is assessed as higher risk. Family members and close associates receive the same treatment as the PEP. The exam loves to test the distinction between foreign and domestic PEPs and whether enhanced measures are automatic or risk-based.

### Recommendation 13: Correspondent Banking ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
FIs should apply enhanced due diligence measures to cross-border correspondent banking relationships.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.13):**

**Core obligations for the correspondent institution:**
1. **Gather sufficient information** about the respondent institution to understand the nature of its business
2. **Determine** from publicly available information the reputation of the institution and the quality of supervision (including whether it has been subject to ML/TF investigation or regulatory action)
3. **Assess** the respondent institution's AML/CFT controls
4. **Obtain senior management approval** before establishing new correspondent relationships
5. **Clearly understand** the respective AML/CFT responsibilities of each institution
6. For **payable-through accounts**: be satisfied that the respondent has conducted CDD on customers having direct access to the correspondent's accounts

**Shell bank prohibition:**
- FIs must **NOT** enter into or continue a correspondent banking relationship with a **shell bank**
- FIs must satisfy themselves that respondent institutions do **NOT** permit their accounts to be used by shell banks

**Shell bank definition:** A bank that has **no physical presence** in the country where it is incorporated and licensed, and is **not affiliated** with a regulated financial group subject to effective consolidated supervision. Physical presence means **meaningful mind and management** -- the mere existence of a local agent or low-level staff is insufficient.

> **Key Exam Point:** Correspondent banking is a classic exam topic. Remember the shell bank prohibition, the requirement for senior management approval, and that "payable-through accounts" require the correspondent to verify that the respondent has done its own CDD. The exam will test whether you understand the layered risk in the correspondent banking chain.

---

### Recommendation 14: Money or Value Transfer Services (MVTS) ★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries must ensure that MVTS providers are **licensed or registered**, and subject to effective monitoring and compliance systems.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.14):**

- MVTS providers (e.g., hawala operators, remittance services) must be **licensed or registered**
- Agents of MVTS providers must also be licensed/registered, OR the MVTS provider must maintain a **current list of agents** accessible to competent authorities
- Countries must take action to **identify unlicensed/unregistered MVTS operators** and apply sanctions
- MVTS providers using agents must **include agents in their AML/CFT programmes** and monitor their compliance
- An FI already licensed to perform MVTS does **not** need a separate license

> **Key Exam Point:** MVTS is a high-risk sector due to cash intensity, rapid transactions, and the potential for informal value transfer (hawala). The exam tests understanding of the licensing/registration requirement and the responsibility of MVTS providers for their agents.

---

### Recommendation 15: New Technologies and Virtual Assets/VASPs ★★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries and FIs should identify and assess ML/TF risks that may arise in relation to new technologies, and should apply a risk-based approach to managing those risks. Countries should ensure that VASPs are regulated for AML/CFT purposes.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.15):**

**2019 Landmark Amendment -- Virtual Assets and VASPs:**

In June 2019, the FATF amended R.15 and adopted INR.15 to bring **virtual assets (VAs)** and **virtual asset service providers (VASPs)** within the scope of AML/CFT standards. This was the single most significant post-2012 change to the FATF Standards.

| Key Term | FATF Definition |
|---|---|
| **Virtual Asset (VA)** | A digital representation of value that can be digitally traded or transferred, and can be used for payment or investment purposes (excludes digital representations of fiat currencies, securities, and other financial assets already covered elsewhere) |
| **VASP** | Any natural or legal person that conducts as a business: exchange between VAs and fiat currencies; exchange between different VAs; transfer of VAs; safekeeping/administration of VAs or instruments enabling control over VAs; participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer/sale of VAs |

**Core VASP obligations:**
- VASPs must be **licensed or registered** (at minimum in the jurisdiction of creation/incorporation)
- VASPs are subject to **all applicable FATF Recommendations** that apply to FIs (including R.10 CDD, R.11 record-keeping, R.20 STRs)
- The **Travel Rule** (R.16) applies to VASPs -- they must collect, hold, and transmit originator and beneficiary information for VA transfers
- Countries must assess and mitigate ML/TF risks from VAs and VASPs
- Countries that choose to **prohibit** VA activities must still have mechanisms to enforce the ban
- **Risk-based supervision** of VASPs is required
- Competent authorities should have the power to apply **sanctions** for non-compliance

**February 2025 Amendment:** INR.15 was also updated as part of the financial inclusion amendments, with the same proportionality language applied to VA/VASP regulation.

> **Key Exam Point:** The 2019 VASP amendment to R.15 is a near-certainty on the CAMS exam. Know: the FATF definitions of VA and VASP; that VASPs are subject to the SAME obligations as traditional FIs (CDD, record-keeping, STRs, Travel Rule); and that VASPs must be licensed or registered. The exam may also test the application of the Travel Rule to crypto transactions.

---

### Recommendation 16: Wire Transfers / The Travel Rule ★★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should ensure that FIs include required and accurate originator information and required beneficiary information on wire transfers and related messages, and that the information remains with the wire transfer throughout the payment chain.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.16):**

#### Information Requirements

| Transfer Type | Originator Information | Beneficiary Information |
|---|---|---|
| **Cross-border wire transfers above USD/EUR 1,000** | Name, account number (or unique reference), address OR national ID OR customer ID OR date and place of birth | Name, account number (or unique reference) |
| **Cross-border wire transfers below USD/EUR 1,000** | Name and account number (or unique reference) -- need not be verified unless ML/TF suspicion | Name and account number (or unique reference) |
| **Domestic wire transfers** | May use account number or unique reference only (full info must be available to beneficiary institution and authorities on request) |

**June 2025 Revision:**
The FATF revised R.16 in June 2025 to adapt to changes in the payments landscape, including new products, market participants, business models, and messaging standards. Key changes include:
- Extending the scope to cover **payment intermediaries** and **payment infrastructure providers**
- For **legal persons**, the name may be supplemented or replaced by a **Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)** or **Business Identifier Code (BIC)**
- Clarified obligations for different roles in the payment chain

**Responsibilities by role:**

| Role | Key Obligation |
|---|---|
| **Ordering institution** | Collect and include required originator/beneficiary information; not execute if information is missing |
| **Intermediary institution** | Ensure all required information accompanies the transfer; risk-based policies for handling transfers with missing information |
| **Beneficiary institution** | Identify transfers with missing/incomplete information; risk-based policies for executing, rejecting, or suspending such transfers |

> **Key Exam Point:** The Travel Rule is fundamental to AML compliance. Know the USD/EUR 1,000 threshold for full information requirements on cross-border transfers; understand that the Travel Rule now applies to VASPs (via R.15); and remember that domestic transfers can use abbreviated information but full details must be retrievable on request.

### Recommendation 17: Reliance on Third Parties

FIs may rely on third parties to perform elements of CDD, but the **ultimate responsibility** remains with the FI. The FI must: obtain the necessary CDD information from the third party immediately; take steps to satisfy itself that copies of identification data will be available on request; and satisfy itself that the third party is regulated, supervised, and applies CDD measures consistent with R.10.

### Recommendation 18: Internal Controls and Foreign Branches and Subsidiaries ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
FIs should implement programmes against ML/TF that include internal policies, procedures, and controls, including compliance management arrangements and screening procedures for hiring employees.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.18):**

**Required programme elements:**
1. **Compliance management arrangements** -- including appointment of a **compliance officer at management level**
2. **Adequate screening procedures** to ensure high standards when **hiring employees**
3. An **ongoing employee training programme**
4. An **independent audit function** to test the system

**Financial group requirements:**
- Programmes must apply to **all branches and majority-owned subsidiaries**
- Must include **information-sharing policies** for CDD and risk management across the group
- Group-level compliance, audit, and AML/CFT functions must have access to customer, account, and transaction information from branches and subsidiaries
- Where the host country's AML/CFT requirements are **less strict** than the home country, branches and subsidiaries in the host country must apply the **home country's higher standard** (to the extent local laws permit)
- Where host country laws prevent implementation, the FI must apply additional measures and **inform the home country supervisor**

> **Key Exam Point:** The exam tests the "four pillars" of an AML compliance programme: (1) internal policies/procedures/controls, (2) designated compliance officer, (3) training, (4) independent audit. Also know the rule that branches/subsidiaries must follow the HIGHER standard when home and host country requirements differ.

---

### Recommendation 19: Higher-Risk Countries ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
FIs should apply **enhanced due diligence (EDD)** proportionate to the risks when dealing with natural or legal persons from countries identified by the FATF as higher risk. In the most serious cases, countries should apply **countermeasures**.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.19):**

**Examples of countermeasures:**
- Requiring specific elements of EDD
- Enhanced relevant reporting mechanisms or systematic reporting
- Restricting business relationships or financial transactions with the identified country or persons
- Requiring FIs to review, amend, or terminate correspondent relationships with FIs in the identified country
- Requiring increased supervisory examination for branches/subsidiaries of FIs from the country
- Requiring increased external audit requirements for FI groups with respect to their operations in the country

> **Key Exam Point:** R.19 is the Recommendation that gives teeth to the FATF's grey list and black list. EDD is required for grey-listed countries based on risk assessment; the FATF explicitly calls for countermeasures against black-listed countries. Know the examples of countermeasures listed in INR.19.

---

### Recommendation 20: Reporting of Suspicious Transactions ★★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
If a financial institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity or are related to TF, it should be required by law to **report promptly** to the **Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)**.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.20):**

- The reporting obligation covers **all criminal acts** that would constitute a predicate offence for ML, as well as TF
- Reports should cover **attempted transactions** as well as completed ones
- Reporting in **good faith** should provide protection from liability (safe harbor)
- There is **no monetary threshold** for filing an STR -- suspicion is the trigger
- **Tipping off** (disclosing that an STR has been filed) should be prohibited

> **Key Exam Point:** The STR obligation is triggered by **suspicion**, not by transaction amount. There is NO minimum threshold. Attempted transactions are also reportable. The safe harbor (protection from liability for good-faith reporting) is essential to incentivize compliance. Tipping off is prohibited.

---

### Recommendation 21: Tipping-off and Confidentiality

FIs, their directors, officers, and employees should be protected by law from criminal and civil liability for breach of confidentiality when they file an STR in good faith, **even if they did not know precisely what the underlying criminal activity was**. They should be prohibited from disclosing (tipping off) that an STR has been filed.

---

### Recommendation 22: DNFBPs -- Customer Due Diligence ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
The CDD and record-keeping requirements in R.10, R.11, R.12, R.15, and R.17 apply to **Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs)** in specified situations.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.22):**

| DNFBP Category | CDD Trigger |
|---|---|
| **Casinos** | When customers engage in transactions equal to or above **USD/EUR 3,000** |
| **Real estate agents** | When involved in buying/selling of real estate for clients |
| **Dealers in precious metals and precious stones** | When engaging in any **cash transaction** equal to or above **USD/EUR 15,000** |
| **Lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals, and accountants** | When preparing for or carrying out specified transactions: buying/selling of real estate; managing client money/securities/other assets; management of bank/savings/securities accounts; organization of contributions for creation/operation/management of companies; creation, operation, management of legal persons/arrangements; buying/selling of business entities |
| **Trust and company service providers (TCSPs)** | When preparing for or carrying out transactions for a client in specified capacities |

> **Key Exam Point:** The thresholds differ: casinos = USD/EUR 3,000; dealers in precious metals/stones = USD/EUR 15,000 (cash transactions only). Know which DNFBPs are covered and what triggers their CDD obligations. The exam frequently tests the application of AML requirements to gatekeepers (lawyers, accountants, real estate agents).

---

### Recommendation 23: DNFBPs -- Other Measures

The STR requirements (R.20) and internal controls requirements (R.18) apply to DNFBPs in the same circumstances as R.22. Lawyers, notaries, accountants, and other independent legal professionals should report suspicious transactions when acting in specified capacities (but legal professional privilege applies in certain circumstances).

---

## 8. Group E -- Transparency and Beneficial Ownership (R.24-R.25)

### Recommendation 24: Transparency and Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized, as revised March 2022):**
Countries should ensure that adequate, accurate, and up-to-date information on the **beneficial ownership** and control of legal persons can be obtained or accessed in a timely manner by competent authorities.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.24, revised 2022/2023):**

**Core requirements:**
- Countries must assess the ML/TF risks associated with all types of legal persons created in the country
- Countries must require **legal persons** to obtain and hold adequate, accurate, and current beneficial ownership information
- Countries must establish mechanisms (e.g., a **beneficial ownership registry** or alternative mechanisms) to ensure that BO information is accessible to competent authorities in a timely fashion
- Companies must maintain BO information domestically and make it available to competent authorities
- **Multi-pronged approach:** countries may use a combination of mechanisms (e.g., registry + FI/DNFBP records + company records) to ensure adequate access

**March 2022 Strengthening:**
The 2022 revision significantly strengthened R.24, requiring countries to use either:
- A **public authority or body functioning as a beneficial ownership registry**, OR
- An **alternative mechanism** that enables equally efficient access to BO information

> **Key Exam Point:** The push for beneficial ownership transparency has been one of the FATF's top priorities. The 2022 revisions made registries (or equivalent mechanisms) a near-requirement. Expect questions on WHY legal persons are vulnerable to ML (layering through shell companies) and what a multi-pronged approach to BO transparency looks like.

---

### Recommendation 25: Transparency and Beneficial Ownership of Legal Arrangements ★

**The Recommendation (summarized, as revised February 2023):**
Countries should ensure that there is adequate, accurate, and timely information on express trusts and similar legal arrangements.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.25):**

- **Trustees** must obtain and hold adequate, accurate, and current BO information on: the **settlor**, **trustee(s)**, **protector** (if any), **beneficiaries** (or class of beneficiaries), and any other natural person exercising **ultimate effective control** over the trust
- This information must be available to competent authorities in a timely manner
- Trust information should be kept **up to date** and verified
- Countries should require professional trustees (lawyers, accountants, TCSPs) to maintain this information
- Countries should assess risks associated with different types of legal arrangements (not just trusts)

> **Key Exam Point:** Know the parties to a trust (settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiary) and that the trustee bears the obligation to maintain BO information. Legal arrangements (trusts, fiducias, Treuhand) are tested alongside legal persons (companies, partnerships) as vehicles for ML.

---

## 9. Group F -- Powers and Responsibilities of Competent Authorities (R.26-R.35)

### Recommendation 26: Regulation and Supervision of Financial Institutions ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should ensure that FIs are subject to adequate AML/CFT regulation and supervision and are effectively implementing the FATF Recommendations.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.26):**

**Risk-based supervision:**
- Supervisors must apply a **risk-based approach** to supervision, shifting resources to areas of higher risk
- The **frequency and intensity** of supervision should be based on the ML/TF risk profile of the institution
- Supervisors must have **on-site and off-site** access to all relevant information

**Core banking principle:**
- For banks and entities subject to the Basel Core Principles, the licensing process must, at minimum, require a **fit and proper** assessment of the beneficial owners, controllers, and senior management
- **Criminals or their associates** should be prevented from holding significant/controlling interests or management functions in FIs

**Supervisory resources:**
- Supervisors must have **adequate financial, human, and technical resources**
- Must have sufficient **operational independence and autonomy** to be free from undue influence

> **Key Exam Point:** The exam tests your understanding of risk-based supervision. Supervisors must allocate more resources to higher-risk institutions. Also know the "fit and proper" requirement for bank licensing -- criminals must be kept out of bank ownership and management.

---

### Recommendation 29: Financial Intelligence Units ★★

**The Recommendation (summarized):**
Countries should establish an **FIU** that serves as a national centre for the receipt and analysis of STRs and other information relevant to ML/TF, and for the dissemination of the results of that analysis.

**Key Points from the Interpretive Note (INR.29):**

**Core FIU requirements:**

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Function** | National centre for receiving STRs, analyzing them, and disseminating results to competent authorities |
| **Access to information** | Must have access to the widest possible range of financial, administrative, and law enforcement information, including open-source and commercially held data |
| **Independence** | Must be **operationally independent** -- able to carry out functions free from undue influence or interference |
| **Security** | Must maintain strict confidentiality and information-security rules |
| **Dissemination** | Spontaneous and upon request, both domestically and internationally |
| **Egmont Group** | Should apply for Egmont Group membership for international FIU-to-FIU cooperation |
| **Analysis types** | **Operational analysis** (focuses on specific targets/cases) and **strategic analysis** (identifies trends, patterns, and typologies) |

> **Key Exam Point:** FIUs appear frequently on the CAMS exam. Know that the FIU must be operationally independent, that it conducts both operational and strategic analysis, and that the Egmont Group is the international network facilitating FIU-to-FIU information exchange. The FIU does NOT conduct investigations itself -- it analyzes and disseminates.

---

### Recommendations 27-28, 30-34 (Brief Overview)

| R. | Title | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| **R.27** | Powers of supervisors | Supervisors must have adequate powers to supervise, monitor, inspect, and sanction FIs for AML/CFT compliance |
| **R.28** | Regulation and supervision of DNFBPs | Casinos must be licensed; all DNFBPs must be subject to AML/CFT supervision |
| **R.30** | Responsibilities of law enforcement | Countries should designate law enforcement authorities with responsibility for ML/TF investigations |
| **R.31** | Powers of law enforcement | Law enforcement should have access to all necessary documents and information for investigations |
| **R.32** | Cash couriers | Countries should have measures to detect physical cross-border transportation of currency/bearer negotiable instruments |
| **R.33** | Statistics | Countries should maintain comprehensive statistics on STRs, investigations, prosecutions, convictions, confiscation, and mutual legal assistance |
| **R.34** | Guidance and feedback | Competent authorities and SRBs should provide guidance and feedback to FIs/DNFBPs |

### Recommendation 35: Sanctions ★

Countries should have effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions (criminal, civil, or administrative) for failure to comply with AML/CFT requirements. This applies to both natural and legal persons.

---

## 10. Group G -- International Cooperation (R.36-R.40)

### Overview of International Cooperation Recommendations ★

| R. | Title | Key Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **R.36** | International instruments | Countries should become party to and implement the **Vienna Convention** (1988), **Palermo Convention** (2000), **UN Convention against Corruption** (2003), and **Terrorist Financing Convention** (1999) |
| **R.37** | Mutual legal assistance (MLA) | Countries should provide the widest possible range of MLA rapidly and constructively; should NOT prohibit or impose unreasonable conditions on MLA; should have a **central authority** for effective handling of requests |
| **R.38** | MLA: Freezing and confiscation | Countries should have authority to take expeditious action in response to foreign requests to freeze, seize, and confiscate laundered property, instrumentalities, and proceeds of ML/TF |
| **R.39** | Extradition | ML should be an **extraditable offence**; countries should extradite their own nationals or submit cases for prosecution if they do not extradite |
| **R.40** | Other forms of international cooperation | Competent authorities should be able to provide the widest range of international cooperation rapidly to their foreign counterparts, both spontaneously and upon request; this includes FIU-to-FIU, supervisor-to-supervisor, and law enforcement cooperation |

> **Key Exam Point:** International cooperation is a cross-cutting CAMS theme. Know the four key conventions (Vienna, Palermo, UNCAC, TF Convention), the concept of mutual legal assistance, that ML should be an extraditable offence, and that FIU-to-FIU cooperation (through the Egmont Group) is a critical component of R.40.

## 11. The FATF Grey List and Black List

This is one of the most frequently tested FATF topics on the CAMS exam.

### Formal Terminology

| Common Name | Formal FATF Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Grey List** | **Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring** | Countries with strategic AML/CFT deficiencies that have committed to resolving them within agreed timeframes |
| **Black List** | **High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action** | Countries with significant strategic AML/CFT deficiencies where the FATF calls for enhanced due diligence or countermeasures |

### The FATF's Identification Process (ICRG)

The **International Cooperation Review Group (ICRG)** is the FATF body responsible for reviewing jurisdictions with strategic AML/CFT/CPF deficiencies. The ICRG:

1. Reviews countries' compliance with FATF Standards (based on mutual evaluations)
2. Identifies jurisdictions with strategic deficiencies
3. Develops action plans for grey-listed jurisdictions
4. Monitors progress
5. Recommends listing/de-listing decisions to the FATF Plenary (which meets three times yearly: February, June, October)

### Current Lists (as of February 13, 2026)

**Black List -- High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action (3 jurisdictions):**

| Jurisdiction | Status | FATF Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Iran** | No material progress on action plan since Feb 2020 | FATF calls on members to apply **countermeasures**; heightened concern about proliferation financing |
| **DPRK (North Korea)** | Continued failure to address significant AML/CFT deficiencies | FATF calls on members to apply **countermeasures**; serious WMD proliferation threats |
| **Myanmar (Burma)** | Insufficient progress on action plan | Subject to a **call for action**; if no further progress by June 2026, FATF will consider countermeasures |

**Grey List -- Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring (22 jurisdictions as of Feb 2026):**

Algeria, Angola, Bolivia, Bulgaria, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Haiti, Kenya, Kuwait (added Feb 2026), Lao PDR, Lebanon, Monaco, Namibia, Nepal, Papua New Guinea (added Feb 2026), South Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Vietnam, Virgin Islands (UK), Yemen

### Practical Impact

| List | Impact on FIs | R.19 Link |
|---|---|---|
| **Grey List** | FATF does NOT call for EDD -- but jurisdictions' identification as having deficiencies often triggers **risk-based EDD** by FIs as a practical matter; being grey-listed can result in de-risking, reduced correspondent banking access, and economic costs | Not directly triggered, but risk assessments may warrant EDD |
| **Black List** | FATF **explicitly calls for** EDD and, for the most serious cases (Iran, DPRK), **countermeasures**; FIs must apply R.19 enhanced measures | Directly triggered under R.19 |

> **Key Exam Point:** Know the formal names (not just "grey" and "black" list), the current black-listed countries (Iran, DPRK, Myanmar), and the practical distinction: grey list = increased monitoring with action plans; black list = call for action, potentially including countermeasures. The lists are updated three times per year at FATF Plenary meetings.

## 12. The Mutual Evaluation Process

### What Is a Mutual Evaluation?

A **mutual evaluation** is a comprehensive, peer-reviewed assessment of a country's AML/CFT/CPF framework. It evaluates both **technical compliance** (do the right laws and regulations exist?) and **effectiveness** (are they actually working in practice?).

### The Two Components

| Component | What It Assesses | Rating Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical Compliance** | Whether the necessary laws, regulations, and institutional frameworks are in place | **Compliant (C)**, Largely Compliant (LC), Partially Compliant (PC), **Non-Compliant (NC)** |
| **Effectiveness** | Whether the AML/CFT system is achieving the desired results (11 Immediate Outcomes) | **High (HE)**, Substantial (SE), Moderate (ME), **Low (LE)** |

### The 11 Immediate Outcomes (Effectiveness)

| IO | Topic |
|---|---|
| IO.1 | ML/TF risk is understood and coordinated action is taken |
| IO.2 | International cooperation delivers appropriate information and evidence |
| IO.3 | Supervisors appropriately supervise, monitor, and regulate FIs/DNFBPs |
| IO.4 | FIs/DNFBPs apply preventive measures and report suspicious transactions |
| IO.5 | Legal persons and arrangements are not misused for ML/TF |
| IO.6 | Financial intelligence is used by authorities appropriately |
| IO.7 | ML offences are investigated and offenders prosecuted and sanctioned |
| IO.8 | Proceeds of crime are confiscated |
| IO.9 | TF offences are investigated, terrorists are deprived of assets |
| IO.10 | Terrorists and terrorist organizations are prevented from raising/using funds |
| IO.11 | Persons involved in proliferation of WMD are prevented from raising/using funds |

### Fourth Round vs. Fifth Round

| Feature | 4th Round (2013-2023) | 5th Round (2024-present) |
|---|---|---|
| **Methodology** | 2013 Assessment Methodology | **2022 Assessment Methodology** |
| **Cycle length** | ~10 years | **~6 years** (significantly shorter) |
| **Focus** | Balanced technical compliance and effectiveness | **Greater emphasis on effectiveness**, major risks and context |
| **Follow-up** | Structured follow-up process | **Results-oriented follow-up** |
| **CPF** | Limited CPF assessment | **Enhanced CPF assessment** (IO.11 expanded) |
| **Immediate Outcomes** | 11 IOs | 11 IOs (but with updated 2022 methodology criteria) |

> **Key Exam Point:** The mutual evaluation is the enforcement mechanism of the FATF system. Countries that perform poorly can be grey-listed. Know the two components (technical compliance + effectiveness), understand that a country can have good laws (compliant) but poor implementation (low effectiveness), and be aware that the 5th Round began in 2024 with a shorter cycle and greater emphasis on real-world results.

## 13. Recent Updates -- Critical for Current Exams

### February 2025: Financial Inclusion Amendments

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **"Commensurate" replaced with "proportionate"** | Throughout the Standards; defined as measures that appropriately correspond to the level of identified risk |
| **Simplified measures encouraged** | Explicit requirement for countries to allow AND encourage SDD for lower-risk scenarios |
| **Non-face-to-face no longer automatically higher risk** | Only considered potentially higher-risk when appropriate mitigation is NOT in place |
| **Scope** | Amendments to R.1/INR.1, INR.10, INR.15, and Glossary definitions |
| **Purpose** | Address the ~1.4 billion unbanked people globally by removing barriers to financial inclusion |

### June 2019: R.15 VASP Extension

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Virtual assets and VASPs brought into scope** | VASPs subject to same AML/CFT obligations as FIs |
| **Travel Rule extended to VASPs** | R.16 information requirements apply to VA transfers |
| **Licensing/registration required** | VASPs must be licensed or registered |
| **2021 Updated Guidance** | Clarified VA/VASP definitions, stablecoin treatment, peer-to-peer risks |
| **Ongoing implementation gap** | As of 2025, many jurisdictions have still not fully implemented R.15 for VASPs |

### June 2025: R.16 Revision (Payment Transparency)

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Extended scope** | Now covers payment intermediaries and payment infrastructure providers |
| **Legal entity identification** | LEI and BIC recognized as acceptable identifiers for legal persons |
| **Adapted to modern payments** | Reflects evolution of payment business models, products, and technologies |

### March 2022: R.24 Revision (Beneficial Ownership)

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Strengthened BO requirements** | Countries must ensure competent authorities have access to adequate, accurate, up-to-date BO information |
| **Registry or equivalent required** | Public authority/registry or alternative mechanism mandated |

## 15. High-Yield Exam Summary -- "Must Know" Facts

> **Key Exam Points -- Final Review Checklist**
>
> 1. The **Risk-Based Approach (R.1)** is the foundation of the entire FATF framework. Every other Recommendation is implemented through the lens of risk.
>
> 2. **CDD (R.10)** has four elements: (a) identify/verify customer, (b) identify beneficial owner, (c) understand purpose of relationship, (d) ongoing monitoring. The threshold for occasional transactions is **USD/EUR 15,000**.
>
> 3. **Beneficial owner** = the natural person(s) who ultimately owns or controls a customer. For companies, typically 25%+ ownership triggers identification.
>
> 4. **PEPs (R.12):** Foreign PEPs ALWAYS require EDD. Domestic PEPs require EDD only on a risk basis. Family members and close associates are treated the same as the PEP.
>
> 5. **Record keeping (R.11):** Minimum **5 years** for transactions (from the transaction) and CDD (from end of relationship).
>
> 6. **STRs (R.20):** No monetary threshold. Triggered by suspicion. Covers attempted transactions. Good-faith reporting provides safe harbor. Tipping off is prohibited.
>
> 7. **Correspondent banking (R.13):** Requires senior management approval, assessment of respondent's AML/CFT controls, and shell bank prohibition.
>
> 8. **Travel Rule (R.16):** Full originator/beneficiary information required for cross-border transfers above **USD/EUR 1,000**. Now applies to VASPs.
>
> 9. **VASPs (R.15):** Must be licensed/registered. Subject to all FI obligations including CDD, record-keeping, STRs, and the Travel Rule. Added June 2019.
>
> 10. **Internal controls (R.18):** Four pillars -- policies/procedures, compliance officer, training, independent audit.
>
> 11. **DNFBPs (R.22):** Casino threshold = **USD/EUR 3,000**. Precious metals/stones = **USD/EUR 15,000** (cash only). Lawyers/accountants triggered by specific gatekeeper activities.
>
> 12. **Black list** (formal: High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action): Currently Iran, DPRK, Myanmar. **Grey list** (formal: Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring): 22 jurisdictions as of Feb 2026.
>
> 13. **Mutual evaluations** assess two things: technical compliance (do laws exist?) and effectiveness (do they work?). The 5th Round began in 2024 with a shorter 6-year cycle.
>
> 14. **FIUs (R.29):** Operationally independent. Receive STRs. Conduct operational and strategic analysis. Disseminate to competent authorities. International cooperation via the **Egmont Group**.
>
> 15. The **TF offense (R.5)** does NOT require proceeds of crime -- funds can be legitimate. It does NOT require a link to a specific terrorist act.
