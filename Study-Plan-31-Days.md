## Study Strategy

### Core Principles
1. **Understand concepts, don't memorize** -- The exam tests application through scenarios, not recall of definitions
2. **Focus on "why" not just "what"** -- Know why a rule exists, not just that it exists
3. **Red flags are gold** -- The red flags section (Domain III) is one of the most heavily tested areas
4. **Case examples = exam scenarios** -- The case examples in the study guide mirror the exam's scenario-based questions
5. **FATF Recommendations are foundational** -- The study guide itself says these deserve "particular study"

### Daily Routine (Recommended: 2-3 hours/day)
- **First 30 min:** Review flashcards/glossary terms from previous day
- **Main block (60-90 min):** Read condensed study guide chapter + reference full PDF for depth
- **Last 30 min:** Practice questions or self-quiz on the day's material

## Exam Day Tips (May 1)

### Before the Exam
- Arrive 30 minutes early
- Bring valid government-issued photo ID
- Eat a good meal, stay hydrated
- No study materials allowed in the testing room

### During the Exam
- **Time management:** 210 minutes / 120 questions = ~1.75 min per question
- **First pass:** Answer everything you know confidently, flag uncertain ones
- **Second pass:** Return to flagged questions
- **Multi-select strategy:** For 4-Pick-2 questions, evaluate each option independently as true/false rather than comparing options against each other
- **No penalty for guessing:** NEVER leave a question blank
- **Read carefully:** The exam uses "MOST likely," "BEST," and "LEAST likely" -- pay attention to qualifiers
- **Scenario questions:** Identify the red flags first, then determine what action is required
- **When in doubt:** Think about what a compliance officer SHOULD do, not what they COULD do
- **Eliminate wrong answers:** Even narrowing from 4 to 2 options doubles your odds

### Common Traps
- Confusing CDD vs. EDD triggers
- Mixing up structuring vs. microstructuring
- Forgetting that TF can use legitimate funds (unlike ML which launders criminal proceeds)
- Confusing correspondent banking with payable-through accounts
- Not knowing which FATF Recommendation covers which topic
- Mixing up SAR timing requirements across jurisdictions
